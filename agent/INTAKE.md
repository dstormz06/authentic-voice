# Authentic Voice : Intake

The quality ceiling of the copy is set here, not in the drafting. A model cannot invent a
person's real specifics, and every specific it is given is one it does not have to fake.

Send this to the person, or work through it yourself against their repo and prior writing.
Answer what you can. **"I don't know" and "skip" are useful answers**: they tell the writer
where to put a bracket instead of a guess.

---

## Ground truth (required)

1. **What is this for?** Personal site, bio, README, profile, something else.
2. **Who reads it?** Recruiters, clients, other developers, friends, strangers who found it.
3. **What should they do or think afterwards?** One sentence.
4. **How high are the stakes?** Job hunting and fundraising cap the self-mockery. Say so now.

## The facts nobody else has (the important part)

5. **How long have you actually been doing this, and since when?** A year is fine. "Since
   2022" beats "years of experience" every time.
6. **What are you genuinely not good at?** This is the single most valuable answer on the
   form. One honest weakness outperforms five strengths.
7. **What have you actually shipped or made?** Include the abandoned ones and say they were
   abandoned. Counts are good: "three finished, two dead."
8. **What went wrong recently?** A bug, a burnt dinner, a rewrite, a thing that still does
   not work. Specific and small is better than dramatic.
9. **What is physically near you while you work?** The plush shark, the broken chair, the
   third monitor that has never worked. Objects are unfakeable.
10. **What do you have an opinion about that some people would disagree with?** Mild is fine.
    "Tabs" is fine. Nothing is not fine.
11. **What is a real number in your life right now?** Projects, years, subscribers, commits,
    hours. Only real ones. If you do not have one, say so and we will use none.
12. **Where do you actually exist online?** Only links that are live and that you maintain.

## Voice

13. **Paste two or three paragraphs you have already written**, from anywhere: a README, a
    message, an old post. This is worth more than any adjective you could give us. It is how
    the dials get set from evidence instead of guesswork.
14. **Do you swear in writing?** Never, sometimes, constantly.
15. **Do you write in lowercase, use slang, use emoji?** Be honest about what you actually do
    rather than what you think reads well.
16. **Name someone whose writing you like**, or a site whose tone feels right.
17. **What would make you wince to see under your name?** The fastest way to set the ceiling.

## Boundaries

18. **What is off limits?** Employer, location, health, family, age, photographs, anything.
19. **Is there anything you want left vague on purpose?** Vagueness you choose is fine.
    Vagueness the writer invents is the problem.
20. **May we invent anything at all?** Default is no. Say yes only for a parody or anonymous
    page, and then it will be written so it reads as clearly playful.

---

## How the answers get used

Every answer becomes one row in the Fact Ledger with a provenance tag, and no specific
reaches the copy without a row behind it:

| Tag | Source |
|---|---|
| `GIVEN` | You wrote it in the brief |
| `ASKED` | You answered it here |
| `SOURCE` | Recovered from your repo, site or prior writing |
| `PLACEHOLDER` | Nobody knew, so it ships as `[VISIBLE BRACKETS]` and gets reported back |
| `FICTION` | Invented with your explicit permission, on a page that is clearly playful |

There is deliberately no tag for "the writer worked it out." An inference that sounds right is
exactly the kind of detail that gets published and then has to be walked back.

**Minimum viable intake:** questions 1, 5, 6, 7 and 13. With those five answered, the copy has
a real timeline, a real weakness, real work, and evidence for the register. Everything else
improves it.
